Dear Editors-in-Chief,

We submit the manuscript ``Contextual Input versus Representation Sharing in Multi-Metric Runtime Forecasting: Selective Gains and a Recurring Sharing Cost'' for consideration as a Full Length Article in Future Generation Computer Systems.

Engineers who forecast runtime telemetry for cloud resource management must decide two things: whether a target's forecaster should receive other resources' telemetry, and whether several resource targets should share one model. These decisions are usually made together. The manuscript separates them with a 2$\times$2 design (independent or shared models, own-resource or all-resource input) and evaluates CPU, throughput, memory and disk-write forecasting on TimeTrack development data, the Bitbrains fastStorage and Rnd traces and three dependent Materna temporal blocks. A second, separately frozen part repeats the design for LSTM, DLinear, PatchTST-style and iTransformer-style backbones and adds descriptive mechanism diagnostics.

The main findings are that contextual input gives small, selective and model-dependent benefits, whereas representation sharing carries a small but recurring accuracy cost: in typical cells the shared model was less accurate in 30 of 32 backbone, resource and environment cells (13 by more than 1\%, mostly for the LSTM), while larger aggregate penalties came from a few high-scale machines. We position the design against channel-independent versus channel-dependent forecasting, which changes input scope and sharing together. In the shared LSTM, task gradients were close to orthogonal without excess conflict but strongly unequal in magnitude. A separately frozen loss-balancing amendment then made the shared LSTM slightly more accurate in every resource and environment cell, but it did not remove the sharing cost. We believe the work fits FGCS's scope on distributed dynamic resource management, monitoring tools and cloud infrastructures, because it gives practitioners evidence-based guidance on input selection and model consolidation for multi-resource forecasting.

The tier-1 experiment, tier 2 and its loss-balancing amendment were each frozen before execution, and later sensitivity controls are labelled post hoc throughout. The code is public at a tagged commit, and the derived analysis outputs, including those of the loss-balancing amendment, are deposited on Zenodo (doi:10.5281/zenodo.24339239 and doi:10.5281/zenodo.24353235); third-party traces remain available from their original archives. Generative AI use is disclosed in the manuscript as required by Elsevier's policy.
