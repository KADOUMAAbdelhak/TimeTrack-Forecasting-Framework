# Publication Scope and Novelty Audit

## Scope assessment

### Scope A: multi-dataset empirical boundary study

This is the recommended path. Its contribution would be empirical and methodological: a prospectively matched, leakage-safe test of when cross-resource context and naive sharing do or do not help across TimeTrack and two independent Bitbrains environments, with Materna as exposed secondary replication. Advantages are direct continuity with the frozen evidence, no invented architecture, and a credible negative-transfer/efficiency boundary. Risks are protocol adaptation across sampling rates, different physical units, public-trace age, and substantial compute.

### Scope B: new method after external development

Not currently justified. External outcomes might motivate a mechanism, but using Bitbrains to develop that method would consume its pristine status and require another untouched four-resource source for confirmation. No such source is presently secured. This scope must not be entered merely because a new architecture appears more novel.

### Scope C: pivot away from multi-resource forecasting

Premature now because Bitbrains is structurally compatible. Pivot becomes rational if the prospective external protocol cannot be executed/validated, if both independent environments make the boundary scientifically uninformative, or if venue-level novelty review finds the empirical contribution insufficient. Pivoting avoids escalating compute and post-hoc method search but abandons a coherent frozen question before independent testing.

## Genuine contribution versus volume

Potentially new as a package: a real-time multi-horizon, common-support S1/S2/M1 factorial; resource-specific native-unit evaluation; explicit negative-transfer and efficiency boundaries; environment-level independent replication; and a prospective separation of pristine confirmation from exposed replication. The claim is about the limits and heterogeneity of cross-resource learning, not a novel LSTM.

Not novel by itself: training many baselines, using LSTMs, applying log1p/robust scaling, reporting standard forecast metrics, adding public datasets, or producing many tables. Phase 3 post-hoc pockets remain exploratory and cannot be upgraded by narrative.

## Minimum evidence before manuscript writing

1. Freeze the exact external protocol, selected IDs, support/fold hashes, raw archive hashes, hypotheses, analysis, and stopping rules before training.
2. Complete and validate both Bitbrains environments on the same prospective 35-VM design with all expected fits and no test-controlled choice.
3. Report H1-H3 separately by environment and resource, with matched support, seed averaging, paired entity-clustered uncertainty, and an equal-environment synthesis.
4. Demonstrate that conclusions are not artifacts of invalid rows, duplicate handling, unit inversion, or one environment.
5. Preserve Materna as clearly secondary; use it only after the Bitbrains analysis is locked, or omit it.
6. Obtain a final literature and license/acknowledgement review before publication claims or redistribution.

Until items 1-4 exist, the project has a strong experimental foundation and external-replication potential, but is not manuscript-ready.
